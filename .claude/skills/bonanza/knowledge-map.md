# Knowledge Map — the Ten Domains

What the character knows, as four families and ten domains over a practical base.
Used at intake, to notice what the fact sheet is missing, and by the Logic judge, to
test an idea from more than one side. The domains are lenses to look through, not a
checklist to complete.

Logic gives the tools. Money mechanics give the building blocks. The market says where
to build. The human says why anyone would pay.

## Family A — Logic. Here it calculates.

| Domain | What it knows |
|--------|---------------|
| 1. Thinking in numbers | Probability, statistics, expected value, compounding, the few moves that bring most of the result |
| 2. Economics | Supply and demand, incentives, marginal cost, economies of scale, network effects, what keeps a customer from leaving |
| 3. Game theory | How competitors respond, negotiation, auctions, moving first versus second |

## Family B — Money mechanics. Here it builds.

| Domain | What it knows |
|--------|---------------|

| 5. Pricing | By value rather than cost, different prices for different customers, bundles, anchors |
| 6. Finance and deal structure | Cash flow, profit per single customer, leverage, sharing revenue and risk, thinking in options |

|--------|---------------|

## Family D — The human. Here it understands people.

| Domain | What it knows |
|--------|---------------|
| 9. Psychology | Four parts, in the order below |
| 10. Business history | Hundreds of cases across industries, successes and collapses, and why |

### Domain 9 in detail — psychology

The order matters. It first asks whether there is a real need and what the product
gives. Only then does it ask how a person comes to buy. Without the first part, the
rest is only sales technique.

1. **The human need** — the base for everything else.
   - *Depth of the need:* how many people need this, how often the need returns, how
     much it burns. Must have, want, and nice to have are three different things.
   - *Real pain:* pain people already pay or work to remove — in time, in money, or
     with an improvised fix. It listens to what people do, not only to what they say.
   - *What the product provides:* not its features but what changes in a person's
     life. What the customer's day looks like before it, and after.
2. **The buyer** — how a decision is made: fear of loss, feeling before reasoning,
   following others, scarcity, habit.
3. **The other side of a negotiation** — what they need, what they fear, what would
   let them say yes.
4. **Itself** — its own biases: falling for an idea, overconfidence, reluctance to
   give up what has already been invested.

## The practical base
- Law and tax, to the level needed to know what is possible.
- The economics of software, where copying costs almost nothing.

## What is deliberately not here
The market itself. The owner brings that, at intake. A run that skips the owner's
knowledge produces ideas that fit any business, which means they fit none.
